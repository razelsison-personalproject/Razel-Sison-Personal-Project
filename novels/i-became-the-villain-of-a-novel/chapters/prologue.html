PROLOGUE • I BECAME THE VILLAIN OF A NOVEL

The Inception

← PREV INDEX NEXT →

I had just gotten off work, exhausted and ready to go home. The streets of our barangay were packed with young and old celebrating the festival. Bright lanterns swung from every corner, their light flickering like tiny stars against the evening sky. The smell of grilled meat and sweet snacks floated through the air, mixing with laughter and chatter from the crowd.

Families and friends strolled from stall to stall, their voices rising over the sound of children playing and music blaring from a distant stage. The ground beneath my bicycle wheels vibrated with the energy of the festival. But all I wanted was to get through it as fast as possible.

My heart hammered in my chest, not from excitement, but from the crowd pressing in around me. People brushed past, their smiles wide and carefree; a sharp contrast to the tight knot of unease twisting in my stomach.

It's too crowded, I thought as my brows drew together.

With a sigh, I took a turn on a narrow alleyway I usually go when I want to avoid people in the main street. Instead of pushing through the busy main road, I decided to take a very, very long detour; a dark alleyway barely anyone used.

The alley stretched ahead in a narrow path. A cool breeze swept through, carrying the faint sound of cicadas and the rustle of dry leaves. Shadows reached along cracked walls, and the pavement was uneven causing my tires to go unbalanced when it hit uneven surface road.

The festival was far behind me now, but something else waited ahead.

A tiny stall sat tucked in the shadows, piled high with old books that smelled of dust and forgotten stories. An old woman sat behind the table, her silver hair tied loosely, her eyes sharp and watching.

As I pedalled my bike closer, the old woman gestured with her hand beckoning me to come.

I wanted to ignore her but I didn't want to be rude towards an older woman out of respect for her age and her line of work. Also, she might be in need of assistance which is why she called for me, and I'm the only person who decided to pass this alley after all.

Thinking this way, I sighed in resignation as I slowed, which earned a soft knowing smile from the old woman.

I clenched my breaks and stopped just right in front of the table piled with books. “Granny, I notice you waving from afar, do you need help with anything?” I asked, taking a better look at the elder before me.

The old woman shook her head then with a furrowed brow, she then said, "It's unusual to see a woman your age crossing this alley. Is the main road that crowded?"

A soft smile of relief broke out of my face, hearing that the older woman was just concerned about my safety. “Well, I fear I would accidentally hit someone if I push my way over there so I took a detour here,” I replied.

The old woman nodded understandably. “Hm, I see…well, since you're here and it's currently a festival, let me give you something, hold on, now where was that book…hmmm,” her words trailed, reaching beneath the table, she pulled out a single book. The cover was worn leather, cracked and soft from years of handling. The edges were frayed, like it had been opened and closed a thousand times. "Aha…! Here it is," she said, holding it out to me.

I blinked, caught between suspicion and curiosity.

"Take it, this is a gift from fate," she urged, voice gentle but firm. "Read it... and your life will never be the same."

Her gaze held mine like a steady bottomless water. I smiled wryly and found it difficult to reject the elder before me. I reached out and slowly grabbed the book with both hands.

The old woman leaned back in her chair, closing her eyes as if exhausted. Before I could ask her anything else, she was fast asleep, breathing slow and even.

I looked at the book, finding it strange. But since it was a gift, I couldn't just throw it away nor reject her kindness. But I couldn't just take it for free as well. It feels like I'm taking advantage of her.

Rubbing the back of my neck, I stood there watching the old woman let out a loud snore in the quiet alley. With another sigh, I pulled a thousand peso bill from my pocket and placed it quietly on the dusty wooden table. "Thank you," I whispered to her sleeping face.

With the mysterious book tucked beneath my arm, I mounted my bicycle and pedalled away from the alley. The vibrant festival lights and the lively noise slowly disappeared behind me, swallowed by the silent embrace of the night. The old woman's face lingered for a moment in my mind, then gently faded like a soft whisper carried away by the wind.

That night, sitting on the edge of my bed, the book felt heavier than I expected. I flipped through its yellowed pages under the soft glow of my lamp.

The story was familiar.

The villain was too predictable and the plot followed a cliché troupe. The usual story where masculinity saves the day, while the female partner was harassed over and over again by the antagonist.

I yawned. “Whelp, tomorrow is another day and I have work tomorrow so…let's go to sleep.”

Closing the book, I rubbed my tired eyes and leaned back, feeling an odd warmth growing inside me. Maybe the story was cliché and boring, but somehow it felt like something about it was oddly familiar. Like a distant forgotten memory.

I shrugged then switched off the lamp, pulled the blanket over myself, and closed my eyes, ready for sleep.

Just as I was drifting off, a soft buzzing filled the air. Faint at first, then growing louder, like a thousand bees fluttering close.

My heart grew suspicious and immediately opened my eyes. The room around me blurred and stretched through colors swirling and twisting like smoke. When everything finally settled, I wasn't in my room anymore.

"Lady Nasrin, please wake up or you will be late for the registration of the Mystic Academy..."

I blinked several times, trying to clear the fog from my mind. As my vision sharpened, I saw a middle-aged woman standing beside my bed, dressed neatly in a maid's outfit. The sunlight streaming through the tall, arched window painted golden patterns across the richly furnished room. Heavy wooden furniture, embroidered tapestries, and a thick rug gave the space an ancient charm.

This is not my room, I thought in my head as my brows almost crossed together.

My heart slowly but increasingly hammered against my ribs.

I sat up abruptly, the soft linens rustling beneath me.

My eyes widened in shock as I took in the unfamiliar surroundings. "What the actual fuck…?" I whispered, my mouth hanging open.

The maid's eyes met mine, her eyes were calm and steady, as if this moment happened every day. "Lady Nasrin, I have lived a long life but this word 'fuck,’ may I have the meaning of the word, my lady?" she asked, completely serious. “I fear I may not be able to assist you otherwise with this word ‘fuck,’ my lady.”

My mouth dropped open even wider. Nasrin? That name... It couldn't be.

It was the villain's name; the very villain from the cliché novel I'd just finished reading.

"Oh no," I breathed, scrambling out of bed.

My legs felt strange, my skin felt smooth to the touch like porcelain. Panic surged my heart, I stood up and stumbled toward the full-length mirror on the wall. I tripped over my own feet and fell hard onto the polished wooden floor.

Groaning, I looked down at my legs which are now slender and long, nothing like my usual short ones with lean muscles from biking everyday.

“I'm definitely not in my own body,” I muttered under my breath.

"Lady Nasrin, are you alright?" The maid hurried over, her voice full of concern as she helped me up. "You suddenly ran, and I was worried something was wrong."

"Yeah, thanks... granny," I whispered, brushing myself off and trying to calm my racing heart.

"Granny?" she repeated, her tone puzzled. “I fear my knowledge is lacking and may not be able to assist you further, my lady, would you rather have me call Chamberlain Lory?”

I couldn't hear anything more as I kept my concentration on the unfamiliar figure in front of me, moving slowly toward the mirror. My reflection stared back at me.

Long dark hair falling past my shoulders, bright brown eyes that gleamed like the sun, and a slim almost model-like figure. The face almost looked like me but unmistakably this one appeared like a celebrity.

I pinched my cheek hard. Pain spread from the nerves of my pinched cheek. “Not a dream…?” I mumbled to myself, utterly confused.

"Lady Nasrin...?" The maid's voice was gentle and worried.

I saw her gaze through the mirror and swallowed hard. "This may sound weird but..." I felt a lump on my throat as I swallowed. "What is my name, granny?"

She hesitated, confusion flickering in her eyes. Then, with a sigh, she said softly, "My lady, you are the only daughter of Sir Lukas Urduja, heiress of the Urduja clan, Lady Nasrin Urduja."

Her words hit me like a thunderclap.

The villain.

I was inside her body.

Memories of the book flooded my mind. This Nasrin hurt so many people, and what's worse is that she met a terrible end. For a brief moment, I felt my soul almost leave my body.

"Are you not feeling well, my lady? Would you like me to pass a word to Chamberlain Lory and cancel your carriage going to Mystic Academy instead?"

The maid's voice pulled me back. Mystic Academy.

The place where everything started. An idea sparked inside me. Maybe I could change the story…change my fate.

My brows crossed together as a determined resolved slowly settled in me as I stared at myself in the mirror. Then I gazed at granny in the mirror. "Granny, I will change my fate."

She looked at me through the mirror as if I'd sprouted a horn from my forehead. "...?"

End of Prologue
