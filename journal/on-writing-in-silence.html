On Writing in Silence

Process Writing Life First Drafts

Why I turn off the internet, move my desk to face a blank wall, and write every first draft by hand — and why I think it's the only way.

Every novel begins in noise. The world demands attention, notifications flicker across screens, and the temptation to check research references every four minutes creates a stuttering, fragmented thought process.

Three years ago, midway through struggling with the second draft of Daughters of the Fog, I closed every browser tab, unplugged the router, and carried a blank notebook to an empty room. What felt initially like withdrawal slowly transformed into room to think.

"Silence is not the absence of sound; it is the presence of attention."

The Tactile Speed of Handwriting

When typing on a keyboard, your hands move faster than your imagination can thoroughly flesh out a scene. Handwriting forces a cadence that matches deep thought. You cannot delete a sentence; you can only cross it out and keep moving forward.

This friction is intentional. It prevents early self-editing — the single greatest killer of first drafts.
